Here's how transactions.php closes the relay loop.

The paybill's money trail now lands on your domain (callback.php -> mysql). But your
local machines are the opposite problem: they have no public IP, they power off, they
sit behind NAT/CG-NAT. So the domain can never "push" to them - not 100% reliable, not
firewall-friendly.

So we flip the direction: the local machines PULL from the domain.

transactions.php is just a tiny, credentialed SELECT over your own DB:

    GET https://yourdomain/transactions.php?since=<cursor>&limit=1000

- cursor = the newest trans_time the local machine has already synced (stored in a tiny
  state file)
- returns only rows newer than the cursor, oldest-first, JSON
- Basic auth (API_USER/API_PASS), always on HTTPS
- the result is upserted locally by id (the same Safaricom PK), so a re-run can't
  double-count anything

Why this is the right shape:

1. The domain and its DB are always on, so they're the source of truth. Safaricom
   writes there 24/7 whether or not a local laptop is awake.
2. Local machines are readers, not listeners: no static IP, no port forward, no VPN,
   no firewall exceptions - exactly the problem we wanted to avoid.
3. Offline is fine by construction: machine off for a week? Its next pull sends
   everything since its cursor in one go - nothing missed, nothing duplicated.
4. MySQL never leaves your hosting: the local machines only ever see rows through
   this credentialed HTTPS SELECT - your DB creds stay local to your hosting panel.

So the trio is: callback.php = Safaricom's writing clerk (always on),
transactions.php = the librarian who serves copies to whoever asks, with a bookmark,
local workers = the clerks who copy new entries into their notebooks whenever they
show up for work, exactly once.

Once you set API_USER/API_PASS, the local side starts pulling automatically every
60s, and the last piece (register-urls, once you enable M-Pesa C2B on the app) makes
Safaricom feed your callback.php live.
